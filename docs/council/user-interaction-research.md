# User interaction during council: bounded evidence

Research date: 2026-10-25. Design input only; no policy adopted or implementation proposed here. Scope: moderator agenda → independent targeted positions and agenda amendments → item debate → fidelity-checked account → user decisions at the end. Read alongside [agenda-led process research](agenda-led-process-research.md).

## Findings from three primary sources

**Interruption burden is supported; slower task work is not.** Gloria Mark, Daniela Gudith, and Ulrich Klocke, *The Cost of Interrupted Work: More Speed and Stress* (CHI 2008), experimental design, results, and discussion, PDF pp. 2–4: 48 participants performed simulated office email work with no, same-context, or different-context interruptions. Interrupted task work was faster, with no significant difference in measured errors or politeness, and greater reported stress, frustration, time pressure, and effort. Crucially, their task-time measure **subtracts time spent handling interruptions**; it is not total elapsed workflow time. Same-context interruptions did not eliminate the measured burden. The authors suggest compensatory faster working, while acknowledging that finer measurements would be needed to establish immediate reorientation costs. This supports concern about human attention and workload, not a claim that interruptions necessarily slow task execution or reduce quality. The laboratory email task does not establish effects for occasional council clarification. [Primary paper](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~gmark/chi08-mark.pdf).

**Clarification has value as well as a cost.** Eric Horvitz, *Principles of Mixed-Initiative User Interfaces* (CHI 1999), principles 2–5 and 7–9, and “Dialog as an Option for Action”: the framework weighs uncertain user goals, attention, and the costs and benefits of acting, asking, or waiting. It explicitly allows dialog to resolve consequential uncertainty while considering needless disturbance, and recommends limiting the cost of poor guesses and matching service precision to uncertainty. This supplies a conceptual reason both to defer low-value questions and to avoid confident conclusions based on unknown user premises. It is a design framework illustrated by scheduling assistance, not an experiment comparing council interaction policies. [Primary paper](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/chi99horvitz.pdf).

**Traditional ATAM does not validate user-at-end.** Mario R. Barbacci, *SEI Architecture Analysis Techniques and When to Use Them*, CMU/SEI-2002-TN-005 (2002), §§3–3.2, printed pp. 9–13: customer business drivers and the architect's overview inform Phase 1 before analysis; participants prioritize quality goals. Phase 2 brings broader stakeholder scenario generation and further analysis before results are presented. Stakeholders influence the questions and premises during evaluation, rather than merely receiving its account. End-only user interaction is therefore a council adaptation with a stakeholder-input tradeoff, not a practice validated by this ATAM precedent. [Primary report](https://sei.cmu.edu/documents/1990/2002_004_001_13946.pdf).

## Separate the costs

- **Human context switching:** a question can pull the user from other work and require attention to council context. Mark et al. supports workload concerns; it supplies no council-specific delay estimate.
- **Synchronous waiting latency:** if dependent debate blocks until the user replies, elapsed time includes response waiting. This is a structural inference, not an experimental finding. Independent items may continue, and batching does not guarantee lower total completion time.
- **Delayed clarification and rework:** reasoning under a wrong requirement can produce an irrelevant recommendation or require reconsideration. This is a council-specific inference consistent with Horvitz's uncertainty tradeoff; these sources do not measure its frequency or cost.

None of these sources directly studies AI councils or establishes which interaction policy has the best speed, quality, or user experience.

## Candidate recommendation for the user's consideration

For the proposed end-only sequence, ask no mid-debate user questions. First inspect available requirements and context. Label dependent outcomes **conditional**, **open: missing user premise**, or **withheld**, specifying the unanswered premise and how each plausible answer changes the recommendation. Continue independent items; stop speculative debate when only the user can resolve the premise. Preserve those conditions through the fidelity check and collect the necessary user decisions together at the final handoff. After answers arrive, resume only affected items whose premises change; do not rerun the whole council automatically. This contains unsupported conclusions but cannot eliminate rework. Whether that tradeoff warrants an end-only policy remains the user's choice.

## What independent preparation means

Independent position formation means reviewers inspect enough evidence to form their own initial positions before seeing peers' conclusions, with room to amend the agenda. A common agenda still frames their attention. Full repeated change-wide design-discovery passes add a separate breadth requirement: every reviewer searches the entire change for design questions. Conventional correctness/standards PR review instead checks behavior, defects, requirements, and repository rules, although concerns can overlap. These are working scope distinctions, not study results. The sources above do not establish that full repeated discovery passes benefit a council, or settle their value relative to targeted preparation or conventional review.
